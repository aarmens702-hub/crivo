# "jev": what it is, and whether crivo should use it

Research date: **2026-10-14**. Every claim below has a date and a source, and the full source list is at the end. Where a number comes from the vendor, the report says so. "I could not verify" means I could not verify it.

---

## 0. Identification

**"jev" is Jev, from TypeSafe AI.** It launched in limited early access on **2026-09-15**. Jev 1.14.0 shipped **today, 2026-10-14**, as `jev-preview`.

**Confidence: high (~95%).** The spelling matches exactly. The launch was the biggest model-launch story of the past month: #1 on HN with 1,989 points and 520 comments (2026-09-15), and a launch post on X that Latent Space reported at 4.21M views. Vercel called it the fastest-adopted model in AI Gateway history. It is marketed on speed, and the vendor claims "70–500 ms" responses. A follow-up release went out today. All of that fits "came out fast and quick".

**The catch: Jev is not an LLM in the sense crivo needs.** TypeSafe calls it a "System One model", and the press calls it a decision model. You give it a `state` (text or JSON) and a set of typed questions: `choice`, `score`, `noul` (a yes/no probability), and on 1.14 also `box`. It returns typed answers with probabilities. **It does not generate text or code and cannot hold a conversation.** TypeSafe's own docs say so (docs.typesafe.ai, "Jev with coding agents", read 2026-10-14). It is not served on a chat-completions endpoint.

## TL;DR

- **What it is.** Jev is a fast, cheap, proprietary classifier/router/scorer. You give it a state and typed questions, and it returns calibrated-ish probabilities in roughly **0.24–0.36 s** (independent measurements) at **$0.042 per million input tokens, with output free** (2026-10-14).
- **It can't drive crivo.** crivo's CLEAN and QUERY loops need a model that writes pandas inside `<execute>` tags. Jev can't write anything. And `generate()` calls `chat.completions.create`, an endpoint Jev isn't served on. **Setting `CRIVO_MODEL=typesafe/jev-1.13` will not work.**
- **Jev Router is the one Jev-branded option crivo can call today.** It works through the existing OpenAI path. But it hands each turn to an LLM it chooses, and crivo's provenance stamp records "typesafe/jev-router", not the model that actually answered. That breaks the "every answer ships with provenance" contract. Not recommended for bench or provenance-bearing runs.
- **Where Jev could help: narrow sidecar decisions.** The best candidate is a pre-filter for the QUERY-mode intent check (match/mismatch). Later, it could rank skills once the library outgrows disease-id lookup. Both need a labeled eval first. Jev's documented weak spots are numbers, counting, dates read as text, and indirection, which are exactly crivo's domain.
- **Independent quality is good but not dominant.** On JevBench v1.6.1 (2026-10-06), Jev 1.13 has capability 77.1. Several **Apache-2.0** open models score higher, the top being Perplexity Decider v1.1 27B at 82.8. Red Hat (2026-10-02) found decision models, Jev included, "do not reliably outperform LLM-as-a-judge".
- **If the goal is a faster crivo loop, the lever is a faster *generative* model.** crivo's own telemetry (19 DeepSeek V4 Pro calls) shows a median **110 s** per call, with **~1.2K input and ~6.8K output tokens**. The calls are bound by output (decode and reasoning) tokens. The cheapest fair experiment is `CRIVO_MODEL=deepseek-flash` on the native path, then `claude-haiku-5-5`. §Crivo fit has the design.

## 1. Who made it, when, why; architecture

**Maker.** TypeSafe AI, San Francisco, founded 2024. Co-founders: Diogo Almeida (CEO; about four years at OpenAI on RLHF, InstructGPT, ChatGPT and GPT-4), Erik Gafni and Sasha Sheng (Wikipedia, read 2026-10-14; TechTarget, 2026-09-22). The company spent about two years in stealth (TypeSafe, Jev 1.14 post).

**Timeline.**
- 2026-09-15: launch plus a $40M seed led by DCVC (Wikipedia; Latent Space, 2026-09-16).
- 2026-09-18: Vercel reports ~13% of paid AI Gateway teams used Jev within 24 h, during a free promo that ran to 2026-09-25 (Vercel blog, 2026-09-18).
- 2026-09-18: listed on OpenRouter as `typesafe/jev-1.13` (canonical slug `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`).
- ~2026-09-20: waitlist removed (TypeSafe on X). Some reports say signups were paused again on ~2026-09-22. I could not verify which is current.
- 2026-09-25: Jev Router on OpenRouter.
- 2026-10-09: an $870M Series A at a $7.5B valuation, led by a16z with Sequoia and DCVC, and Martin Casado joining the board (Bloomberg via Investing.com, 2026-10-09). The company claims "a third of the Fortune 500" use Jev. a16z's own post reportedly says 25% "integrated" (QUASA). Neither figure is independently verified.
- 2026-10-14: Jev 1.14.0 ships as `jev-preview`. `jev-latest` moves to 1.14.0 "in one week", and 1.13.0 gets a 60-day deprecation notice (TypeSafe, Jev 1.14 post).

**Why.** The thesis is to put cheap, fast, typed decisions inside ordinary software, instead of asking an LLM to "return JSON". The name comes from the Jevons paradox: cheaper intelligence leads to more deployment (Wikipedia).

**Architecture (mostly undisclosed).**
- **Disclosed by TypeSafe:**
  - Non-autoregressive "Parallel" sampler that "generates all outputs in a single query".
  - Trained with **RLCD** ("Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions").
  - Not fine-tuned on customer data; the same weights serve every account.
  - English is primary; other languages are less reliable (launch post and docs/models, read 2026-10-14).
- **Not disclosed:** parameter count; dense vs MoE; base model. TypeSafe's "no standard benchmark tables" policy is in its "Lies, Damned Lies, and Benchmarks" post.
- **Per Wikipedia:** the company calls it transformer-based and trained only on synthetic data. I could not find that statement in TypeSafe's docs.
- **Community inference, unverified:**
  - A backbone LLM plus a linear or pointer readout head over the options, in one forward pass. Kev (Qwen3.5-4B-Base LoRA plus pointer head) and Cloudflare Clef (Qwen3.8-27B plus a joint schema head) reproduce the behaviour this way.
  - An HN commenter (hbrn, 2026-09-22) says Jev "consistently thinks it's Qwen". That is an anecdote, not evidence.
- **Not:** a diffusion LLM (though "Diffusion Jev" and djev clones exist on DiffusionGemma). No Cerebras or Groq hardware is disclosed.
- **Context (docs/models, read 2026-10-14):**
  - Default: 64K per request and 32K per question (state plus one question).
  - With the `TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Long-Context: true` header: 262K for both. TypeSafe describes this mode as "slower and less accurate" and not for production.
  - OpenRouter lists ctx 64,000 and max prompt 32,000.
  - Choice questions: up to 255 options (docs/api). Score questions: 2–10 levels.
- **Modalities:** text. Images are an experimental "Danger Zone" feature; per the 1.14 post, 1.13 "was multimodal all along". The `box` (bounding box) primitive exists only on 1.14.

**Reading the speed claims.** The 40–400x multipliers compare one Jev call against reasoning LLMs doing open-ended generation. HN's ramon156 (2026-09-15) called that "apples-to-oranges unless the LLM baseline is doing comparable work". Against a well-prompted single-token LLM judge, the gap shrinks or disappears: see Red Hat's numbers, and HN's rickintoplace (2026-10-02), who used Qwen3.5-397B with a one-token readout. Neither OpenRouter nor Artificial Analysis publishes Jev latency or throughput. On 2026-10-14, OpenRouter's endpoint stats for `typesafe/jev-1.13` showed `latency_last_30m: null` and `throughput_last_30m: null`, with 100% uptime over the last day.

## 3. Quality

**Vendor evals.** There are no standard benchmarks, by policy. TypeSafe's four-workflow eval uses "the average of GPT-6 Astra and Fable 5.1" as the reference, which TypeSafe says biases it toward OpenAI and Anthropic. It also publishes an "internal intelligence measurement" in the 1.14 post, but gives no numbers in the text.

**Independent evals.**

- **JevBench (Benchmark Heaven, MIT harness).**
  - Size: 534 English decisions, plus a sealed held-out split.
  - v1.3 (2026-09-22): Jev 1.13 ranked **#1, at 74.4**.
  - v1.4.x: **#3–4, at 63.29**, behind 4B open models (Imajev-4B 67.37, Plumb-4B 65.84).
  - **v1.6.1 (2026-10-06):** Jev 1.13 is the reference row. **Capability 77.1** (Intelligence 63.6, Calibration 90.6, Speed 91.5, Cost 54.7), p50 0.239 s.
  - Open-weight rows with *higher* capability: Perplexity Decider v1.1 27B **82.8** (Apache-2.0), Quyet-1.0-Large 81.7, torchcast-decision-27b 81.6, decisio v0.8.0 79.6, Kev 27B **78.9**.
  - On the hosted-API board: Mercury Decide 73.6 (Intelligence 65.9 vs Jev's 63.6, but worse calibration at 81.3).
  - Caveat: the author has an obvious enthusiasm for Jev. In the Show HN post (2026-09-22) he wrote, "I built JevBench because Jev kicks ass."
- **Red Hat Developer (Geada, Misiura, Cyril; 2026-10-02).** Two guardrail tasks:
  - Prompt injection: Jev **86.35%**, vs deberta-v3 classifier 89.01% and Qwen3.6-35B judge 89.31%.
  - Toxicity: Jev **86.20%**, the best in that table, narrowly ahead of DiffusionGemma (85.53%) and Qwen3.6-35B (85.47%).
  - Conclusion, verbatim: "we did not find that decision models produced faster, cheaper, or higher-quality answers compared with LLM-as-a-judge."
  - The article has internal table inconsistencies (some numbers swapped or mis-stated).
- **Practitioner micro-evals (HN; self-reported, small n).**
  - Citation support on ALCE: Jev 1.13 flagged 87.5% of unsupported citations, GLM-5.3-flash with reasoning 83.9%, Qwen3.5-397B single-token 89.3%. Only 56 positives, so the three are "within each other's error bars" (rickintoplace, 2026-10-02).
  - Replacing an agent's guardrail layer: "Same accuracy, 47× cheaper, 30× faster" (deepanshsaxena, 2026-09-23).
  - AnthusAI claims Jev beat open decision models on multi-step reasoning (hard-decisions.anth.us, 2026-10-02).

**Calibration is contested.** Alex Molas (2026-09-23) argues that calibration is data-distribution dependent, so Jev's probabilities are good for ranking but should be recalibrated, e.g. with Platt scaling on a few hundred labels. He cites tweets showing Jev giving a fair coin 0.92 for heads. On JevBench's demo, an HN commenter noted the slop detector gave 86% confidence on a keysmash: "confident and wrong is worse than an LLM that just hedges" (pushpendraw, 2026-09-22).

**Known weaknesses, from TypeSafe's own "Jev 1.13 jaggedness" page (read 2026-10-14):**
- "Jev is not a calculator"; it "does not count reliably".
- It reads dates "as text, not as ordered quantities".
- Weak on hex/RGB and other numeric representations.
- Indirection and double negatives degrade answers.
- Accuracy falls as irrelevant state grows.
- It is not hostile to adversarial content by default.
- Bias toward the first choice option.
- Not trained to generate text.

**Agentic coding, tool use, code execution, pandas/SQL: not applicable.** Latent Space (2026-09-16) says it "cannot code and doesn't reason". TechTarget (2026-09-22) says it cannot "hold a conversation or write code". It does no tool calling in the LLM sense. TypeSafe's "function calling" cookbook only *selects* a function and fills **closed-set** arguments (Literals, sets, bools). Free text, numbers and dates "get no question", and the function's default is used. No evaluation of Jev on pandas or SQL tasks exists that I could find.

## 4. Open-source status

- **Jev itself: closed and proprietary.** No weights; no Ollama, vLLM or SGLang; no quantizations. API only (Wikipedia; docs; OpenRouter `hugging_face_id: null`).
- **ToS (Master Customer Agreement, last updated 2026-09-23):**
  - §2.3(b) bans using the Services or Output to "perform model distillation, train a model to imitate the output of the Services".
  - Bans building a competing product, reverse engineering, and security testing.
  - "AS IS / AS AVAILABLE"; "THE SERVICES MAY PRODUCE INACCURATE OR ERRONEOUS OUTPUT".
  - No SLA.
  - Usage limits are set per Order.
  - No training on customer data without consent. ZDR is available for enterprise.
- **Open lookalikes, if self-hosting or no vendor is required:**
  - Perplexity Decider v1.1 27B (Apache-2.0, HF 2026-10-05).
  - Cloudflare Clef 27B, Clef-flash 9B and Clef Omni 30B-A3B (Apache-2.0, HF 2026-09-30).
  - Kev 4B / 27B (Apache-2.0).
  - Nace.AI Drex v1.5 9B ("other" license).
  - autotrust JEV-* (Apache-2.0, Qwen bases).
  - JevK5 (GGUF available), and many more: JevBench lists 130 open rows.
  - Ollaya ("Ollama for Jev-style decision models", HN 2026-09-25) serves several of them.
  - Caution: autotrust's JEV-9B describes itself as a distilled "student of TypeSafe Jev 1.13". Whoever trained it appears to have run against MCA §2.3(b). Treat its provenance as legally murky.
- **Local hardware (rule of thumb, not measured).**
  - 4B decision models (Kev 4B, JevK5) run on a laptop CPU or GPU. JevBench shows p50 of about 0.02–0.08 s for 4B rows on their hosts.
  - 27B-class models want roughly 24 GB of VRAM at 4-bit, or a 32 GB+ Apple Silicon machine.
  - Per your memory notes, this Mac is RAM-constrained, so local 27B is likely impractical. A 4B model is plausible.

## 7. Practitioner signal

**Launch thread on HN (2026-09-15, item 49717558):**
- **The core caveat.** jacobgold: "This is probably super useful for classification/routing/scoring, but it's nothing like the code generating models we're all using today for code and automation." He also disputes "can't hallucinate": it can't emit an invalid *type*, but it can still be wrong.
- **Positive.** lubujackson: "this is exactly how I am using LLMs in production, to narrowly make choices and return structured data."
- **On the claims.** ramon156: "all claims just sound like marketing terms"; the latency comparison is "apples-to-oranges".

**Willison and the press:**

## Crivo fit

### What crivo needs (from reading the repo, read-only)

- `src/crivo/llm.py`: `generate()` streams `client.chat.completions.create(...)` for both `deepseek` and `openai` providers, and reads only `delta.content` (and `reasoning_content` as a heartbeat). The `claude` path uses `anthropic.messages.create`.
- `src/crivo/prompts.py`: the model must answer with exactly one `<execute>` Python cell or one `<answer>`. crivo **does not use native tool or function calling**; the "tool" is pandas code inside a text tag. QUERY's final cell must end in `assert`s. CLEAN writes `fix_<slug>(df)` plus asserts.
- Other model calls, all through `_generate_scoped`:
  - the intent check (`INTENT_PROMPT`: `<restatement>` plus a `<verdict>match|mismatch</verdict>` plus `<reason>`)
  - harmonize
  - skill proposal
  - the clean report
- **Deterministic by design, not model slots:** `router.py` (grade routing: "a model-authored key… cannot change the route"), `detect.py` (the 22-disease detectors) and `library.candidates()` (skill lookup by disease id).
- **Telemetry** (`bench/results/agent/telemetry/*.jsonl`, 19 DeepSeek V4 Pro calls, 2026-09-05):
  - Median call **110 s** (p90 219 s, max 350 s).
  - Median **1,200 input tokens** (~1,024 of them cache hits) and **6,795 output tokens** (max 21,940).
  - So crivo's latency is dominated by generated (mostly reasoning) tokens: about 62 tok/s effective.
  - The 2026-09-04 flash experiment (`docs/research/2026-09-04-flash-experiment.md`) found v4-flash *not* faster per call (131 s vs 107 s) on n=1, and none of its fixes survived verification.

### Q1. Could crivo use Jev today through the existing OpenAI-compatible path?

**Jev itself (1.13 / 1.14): no.**
- `CRIVO_PROVIDER=openai CRIVO_BASE_URL=https://openrouter.ai/api/v1 CRIVO_MODEL=typesafe/jev-1.13` would send a chat-completions request for a model OpenRouter serves only on `/api/alpha/decisions` and `/api/v1/systemone`.
- Expect an error. **Not live-tested; I used no API key.**
- Even if a response came back, there would be no `delta.content` for the `<execute>` parser.
- No configuration fixes this. Using Jev at all means a separate HTTP call outside `generate()`.

**Jev Router: technically yes, today, with zero code changes:**
```bash
export CRIVO_PROVIDER=openai
export CRIVO_BASE_URL=https://openrouter.ai/api/v1
export CRIVO_API_KEY=$OPENROUTER_API_KEY
export CRIVO_MODEL=typesafe/jev-router
uv run python -m crivo
```

I don't recommend it, for five reasons:
1. **Provenance break.** `model_info()` stamps `typesafe/jev-router` on the answer card. `generate()` never reads the chunk's `model` field, so the model that actually answered is lost. That violates crivo's lineage contract.
2. **Routing controls are unreachable.** `generate()` has no `extra_body` passthrough, so `cost_tier`, `allowed_models` and `excluded_models` can't be set. You get Jev's default policy over OpenRouter's whole pool, which can include $10/$50 models.
3. **Model switching mid-session.** The router may switch models between turns, which discards crivo's stable cacheable system-prompt prefix.
4. **Unproven protocol fit.** Routed models may not honour the one-tag protocol equally. There is no published head-to-head; the 237/423 vs 130 figure is OpenRouter's own claim.
5. **Bench confound.** The bench would measure a moving target.

### Q2. Is it good enough for the CLEAN/QUERY loop?

**No: it is categorically unable.** The loop *is* code generation. TypeSafe's docs explicitly mark "Replacing your coding agent's model: Not supported" (read 2026-10-14).

**Candidate sub-task slots, ranked by fit:**

1. **QUERY intent-check pre-filter (best fit).**
   - The task: given (question, executed code, answer), does the code compute the asked quantity? That's a `noul`, with an optional `choice` over the mismatch kinds INTENT_PROMPT already enumerates (different column / filter / aggregation / subset / over-generalization).
   - The state holds no dataset rows, so it respects the "never put raw rows in a prompt" rule.
   - The gain: if Jev says "match" with high confidence, skip the LLM call. Only call DeepSeek, to get the human-readable `<restatement>`, when Jev's p(mismatch) is high or its confidence is low.
   - The risk: reading pandas code is exactly the "indirection" Jev's jaggedness page warns about. A false "match" silently ships a wrong answer, which is the failure this gate exists to catch. Adopt only on measured mismatch recall (below).
2. **Skill ranking when the library grows (later).** Today `library.candidates(disease)` is an exact disease-id lookup, so there is nothing to rank. If skills ever need matching across diseases, Jev's 255-option `choice` covers `ACTIVE_CAP = 50`. Its vendor cookbook (182 Hermes skills, jev-1.12) is the analogue. That cookbook's test requests were generated by Claude Sonnet 5, so they are easier than real ones.
3. **Ambiguous semantic-type tie-breaks.** Example: "is this column an ID or a quantity?", given the schema plus truncated samples. This is weak: `semantic_types.py` is regex-deterministic and good at it, and Jev is weak on numbers and dates, which are most of crivo's diseases (money strings, mixed date formats, Excel-mangled IDs).
4. **REPL utterance routing** (is this a CLEAN request, a QUERY, or a meta command?). It works, but it's cheap to do with rules.
5. **Do not use Jev for:** grade routing (deterministic on purpose for governance), anything numeric or date-ordered, or the depth-1 skill gate. Admission is test-gated and human-gated, not a model decision.

### Q3. What a fair head-to-head looks like

**A. Jev on the intent check (an offline eval; no bench lane needed).** No QUERY or intent eval exists in `bench/` today; `bench/agent_run.py` is CLEAN-only.

1. **Build a labeled set** of ~100–200 (question, code, answer) triples, roughly 50/50 match and mismatch.
   - Source: real QUERY transcripts (`kind_note="intent"`) plus controlled perturbations of correct code. Swap a column, drop a filter, mean↔median, head(n) instead of all rows, and over-generalized answer text.
   - Freeze it and hash it before any model sees it, the same discipline JevBench uses.
2. **Arms:**
   - (a) DeepSeek V4 Pro with the current INTENT_PROMPT (the baseline)
   - (b) `jev-1.13.0` noul
   - (c) `jev-1.14.0` noul
   - (d) an open decision model (Perplexity Decider 27B or Clef-flash via OpenRouter) to price the vendor-free option
   - (e) Claude Haiku 5.5 with INTENT_PROMPT, a cheap generative judge that keeps the restatement
3. **Primary metric:** **mismatch recall** at the operating threshold. Secondary: precision, p50/p95 latency, $ per check, ECE.
   - Recalibrating Jev's scores with Platt scaling on your labels is a threshold fit, not imitation training. Still, read MCA §2.3(b) before logging Jev outputs for any model training.
4. **Controls:**
   - Randomize option order (Jev leans toward the first option).
   - Pin model versions, because `jev-latest` moves in about a week.
   - Run arms in the same hours (DeepSeek peak/off-peak changes latency and price).
5. **Adoption rule (pre-registered):** Jev pre-filter recall on mismatches ≥ DeepSeek's, *and* the pre-filter lets ≥X% of checks skip the LLM. Otherwise, drop it.

**B. Fast generative models on the CLEAN bench (the experiment that actually targets speed).**

Arms:
- A: `deepseek-v4-pro`, the default
- B: `deepseek-flash` (V4.1 Flash), native, zero new vendor
- C: `claude-haiku-5-5`, native Claude path
- optional D: an OpenRouter fast model (GPT-6 Luna, or Mercury 2.5 as the "pure speed" control)

Jev cannot be an arm.

Procedure:
```bash
# arm A (default). Results land in bench/results/agent/
uv run python -m bench.agent_run --sample 10 --seed 7 --repeat 3 --force
cp -R bench/results/agent bench/results/agent-v4pro      # copy aside: the filename has no provider/model in it

# arm B
CRIVO_MODEL=deepseek-flash uv run python -m bench.agent_run --sample 10 --seed 7 --repeat 3 --force
cp -R bench/results/agent bench/results/agent-v41flash

# arm C (needs ANTHROPIC_API_KEY)
CRIVO_PROVIDER=claude CRIVO_MODEL=claude-haiku-5-5 \
  uv run python -m bench.agent_run --sample 10 --seed 7 --repeat 3 --force
cp -R bench/results/agent bench/results/agent-haiku55

# optional arm D via OpenRouter
CRIVO_PROVIDER=openai CRIVO_BASE_URL=https://openrouter.ai/api/v1 \
  CRIVO_API_KEY=$OPENROUTER_API_KEY CRIVO_MODEL=openai/gpt-6-luna \
  uv run python -m bench.agent_run --sample 10 --seed 7 --repeat 3 --force

uv run python -m bench.compare bench/results/agent-v4pro bench/results/agent-v41flash
```

Bench gotchas I found in the code:
- **`_arm_suffix` omits the provider and model**, which its own docstring admits. Without `--force` plus copying each arm's directory aside, a new model's run *resumes* DeepSeek's files and reports DeepSeek's numbers as its own.
- `RESULTS_DIR` is a module constant, not a flag.
- `_require_key()` only accepts `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. An OpenRouter-only arm exits unless one of those is set.
- The cost column depends on provider-reported usage.
- **Claude path:** `CLAUDE_MAX_TOKENS = 8192` and `temperature=0.0` are hard-coded. DeepSeek's median output is 6.8K tokens and its max 21.9K, so a thinking Haiku may truncate at 8,192. Smoke-test that `claude-haiku-5-5` accepts these parameters before a paid run. Not tested here. Note also that `CLAUDE_DEFAULT_MODEL` is still `claude-opus-5`; the current Opus is `claude-opus-5-5` (Anthropic models page, 2026-10-14).
- Keep `--wall-cap` and `--max-events` identical across arms, and report aborted counts. The default wall cap of 300 s is only ~3 median DeepSeek calls.

What to measure:
- repair F1 and survived-verification rate (the verifier is the quality floor)
- status mix (ok / ok_unchanged / aborted)
- pass^k via `--repeat` and `bench/consistency.py`
- `wall_secs`, calls, `model_wait_s`, `new_work_tokens`
- **output tokens per call**: the real latency driver, per the telemetry

The success bar is already in `specs/2026-09-06-master-plan.md` (Wave 13): median wall-clock per case halved vs the 2026-09-04 baseline, with no loss in survived fixes. That spec also mandates escalation to pro when a check still fires (A2.2).

### Q4. Risks

| Risk | Jev (sidecar) | Jev Router (as driver) |
|---|---|---|
| Quality drop | Weak on code-reading and indirection, numbers and dates (vendor's own page). Independent evals put it at "good, not best" (JevBench 77.1 vs 82.8 open; Red Hat: no better than an LLM judge) | Unknown per turn; it depends on the routed model |
| Tool-call reliability | N/A (typed outputs). The failure is *confident wrong answers*, and calibration is contested | Varies by routed model; no protocol guarantees |
| Provenance / reproducibility | Aliases move (`jev-latest` → 1.14 in ~1 week; 1.13 has a 60-day deprecation). Pin `jev-1.13.0`, and log the response's `model` field | **High.** crivo records "typesafe/jev-router", not the served model |
| Rate limits / availability | Unpublished; 429/529 exist; SDK timeout 10 s; no SLA; early-access posture; signup status is unclear (opened ~09-20, possibly paused ~09-22) | OpenRouter global region only; 503 when the router is down |
| Vendor / licence | Proprietary, "AS IS". The ToS bans distillation and competing products. One more data processor (code and question text leave the host) | Same, plus routed vendors |
| Cost | Trivial ($0.042/M; about $0.00006 per 1.5K-token check) | Opaque (listed `-1`); you pay whatever model is chosen |
| Strategic | Low switching cost. Apache-2.0 open alternatives match or beat it on JevBench, and some run locally | N/A |

### Ranked recommendations

1. **Don't put Jev in crivo's driver seat.** It can't be done, and no config makes it possible. Skip Jev Router for anything that produces an answer card or a bench row.
2. **For a faster loop, run head-to-head B.** Start with `CRIVO_MODEL=deepseek-flash` (no new vendor, MIT, ~2x AA tok/s, ~1/2 the price), then `CRIVO_PROVIDER=claude CRIVO_MODEL=claude-haiku-5-5` (highest AA index in the cheap tier: 43 at 237 tok/s, $0.10/$0.50). Judge on survived fixes and output tokens per call, not tok/s alone. Your own n=1 flash run is the warning: fast is not good unless the verifier agrees.
3. **Optional: try Jev as an intent-check pre-filter, only after eval A.**
   - Pin `jev-1.13.0`, or 1.14.0 once it becomes `jev-latest`.
   - Implement it as a standalone ~20-line `httpx` call (httpx is already a dependency) beside `_intent_check`, not through `generate()`.
   - That touches the loop and prompts, which are Aarmen's hand-written core per CLAUDE.md, so it should be proposed as a diff, not written unasked.
   - Run arm (d) to see whether an Apache-2.0 decision model (Perplexity Decider 27B, Clef-flash) gives the same result with no proprietary vendor. A local 4B model (Kev 4B / JevK5) is the zero-egress option.
4. **Revisit skill ranking with a decision model only** when the library starts matching skills across disease ids. Today there's nothing to rank.
